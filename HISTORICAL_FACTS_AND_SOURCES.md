# Historical basis and limits of reconstruction

Prepared 26 November 2026. The film mixes documented engineering history with an explicitly fictional homeowner, house and scene sequence. Sources support facts, not invented personal anecdotes. Photographs/documents linked here are references, not media automatically licensed for reuse.

## S1 — Chicago Public Library, Chicago Sewers Collection
https://www.chipublib.org/fa-chicago-sewers-collection/
Alternative collection overview: https://www.chipublib.org/archival_post/chicago-sewers-collection/

Relevant historical finding-aid section describes low ground, inadequate drainage, Chesbrough's sewer plan and raising street grades. New sewers could occupy the old surface before fill and pavement covered them. Owners also raised buildings; some whole blocks were lifted. The source notes subsequent pollution and sanitation problems, so the film does not claim the street project solved every water problem.

The full historical section was available in the earlier successful read and is indexed in current search results; a later direct fetch encountered a JavaScript access wall. Use the indexed finding-aid history or the original page when available, without bypassing access controls.

## S2 — Encyclopedia of Chicago, Street Grades, Raising
https://www.encyclopedia.chicagohistory.org/pages/1202.html

Describes street raising in stages in the 1850s for drainage/sewerage; owners were responsible for adapting buildings. Some properties were not lifted. Height increases varied by location. The film gives no universal lift height for the real city and does not compress the whole project into one literal day.

## S3 — National Park Service / Michigan Technological University
Historic Resource Study of Pullman National Monument, 2019; section 2.C.2, printed pp. 37–38, PDF pp. 54–55 (zero-based page indices 53–54).
https://www.nps.gov/pull/learn/management/upload/Pullman_HRS_Access3-2-1-131_compressed_508-compressed.pdf

The study describes Pullman and partners raising a Lake Street block in 1860, reporting six thousand jackscrews and continuing business. The narration attributes that scale to historical accounts. It does not claim all 6000 jacks are present under our fictional house, or that every building lift was equally uneventful. No verbatim historical quotation is used.

The caption on a neighbouring historical illustration gives a different year from the narrative example. Our on-screen Lake Street 1860 insert is an original schematic reconstruction of the narrated example, not a claim to reproduce that particular illustration.

## S4 — TeachEngineering, University of Colorado Boulder
Just Plane Simple: screw mechanics and mechanical advantage.
https://www.teachengineering.org/lessons/cub_simp_machines_lesson02

Supports rotation-to-translation explanation and force/distance trade-off. The screw's thread advances axially relative to its mating thread. Animate a functioning screw/nut pair; spinning a decorative handle with no relative thread motion does not lift a load. Real friction and limits remain; do not imply free energy or unlimited capacity.

## Do not add

- A claim that the entire city was raised simultaneously, everywhere by one fixed height.
- A claim that the mascot invented the method or personally worked with a named historical engineer.
- Invented quotations from Chesbrough/Pullman or an unsupported disaster/near-collapse presented as documented fact.
- Modern hydraulic equipment in the historical work scene.
- A claim that the 1850s street project instantly cleaned the river or ended all flooding and disease.
- Exact structural ratings, jack dimensions, support spacing or DIY lifting instructions. The film explains a historical principle, not how to perform a real building lift.

Illustrative lift height 1.40 m, set dimensions, repeated crew characters, signals, props and camera paths belong to the production model. They are not archival measurements.
